Typical sizes people reported after the macOS 27 (Golden Gate) update. Yours will differ; the commands below give your own numbers.

You updated to macOS 27, and afterwards Storage settings showed 15 GB less free space, and a "System Data" bar that had grown by twice that. Threads about it are all over r/mac and r/MacOS this week, including one Mac mini reporting 400 GB of System Data out of nowhere. The good news: almost none of it is the operating system. It is leftovers of the update process, and most of it is one file you can delete in ten seconds.

1. The snapshot macOS took before upgrading

Before a major update, macOS takes an APFS snapshot of your startup disk so it can roll back if the install fails. If Time Machine is on, there is an hourly local snapshot too. Each one pins every file that existed at that moment, including the entire previous macOS. On a busy Mac that is 8 to 20 GB, and it counts as System Data because there is no folder for it.

macOS is supposed to delete these within about 24 hours. It often doesn't. List them:

tmutil listlocalsnapshots /

You will see lines like com.apple.TimeMachine.2026-09-19-183012.local. Anything dated before your update is the culprit. Delete it by its date:

sudo tmutil deletelocalsnapshots 2026-09-19-183012

One line you must leave alone: com.apple.os.update-…. That is the sealed system you are booted from, not a backup, and tmutil will refuse anyway. Everything else in the list is fair game once you are happy with the update. Your real Time Machine backups on the external disk are not affected.

2. Apple Intelligence models

On Macs that support it, the update turns Apple Intelligence on and downloads its on-device models into /System/Library/AssetsV2. Reports put it around 7 GB. If you use it, that space is the price. If you don't, turn it off in System Settings → Apple Intelligence & Siri and macOS removes the models; give it a little while and a restart. You cannot delete them by hand, the folder is protected.

3. Caches and the Spotlight index

A major update invalidates every app cache and rebuilds the Spotlight index from scratch. For a few days the disk churns and System Data floats up and down by a few GB. This settles on its own. Clearing ~/Library/Caches now just makes the apps rebuild it again.

4. Update staging files

/Library/Updates holds the downloaded update payload, usually under 1 GB after a successful install, sometimes 3 GB. Software Update cleans it up. Deleting it by hand can leave Software Update confused about what it already has, so leave it.

5. The OS really is a bit bigger

Each major macOS adds a couple of GB to the sealed system volume. That shows under "macOS" in Storage settings, not System Data, and there is nothing to do about it.

The Storage pane is lying to you anyway

Storage settings is a cached estimate. Right after an update it can sit on "Calculating…" for hours, show System Data at three times its real size, or report 50 GB used on a disk that is clearly fuller than that. Do not make decisions from it this week. The real number, in Terminal:

df -h /

"Avail" is what you can actually write. Finder's "available" adds purgeable space to it, which macOS frees on its own when something needs room, so those two numbers disagree by design.

If it's 100 GB or more, look for a leftover volume

The 400 GB Mac mini case is not caches. Two things make System Data jump by hundreds of GB on one machine and not another:

diskutil apfs list

Look for a second "Data" volume in the container. If the Mac ever had a clean install, a beta, or a second macOS on it, the old Data volume can still be there, and Storage settings counts every byte of it as System Data. Then:

sudo du -xsh /private/var/* 2>/dev/null | sort -h | tail -5

which shows the biggest things under /private/var, where update leftovers, swap and logs live. A single 100 GB entry there is a crashed installer or a runaway log, both safe to remove once you know which.

What not to do

  • Don't reinstall macOS to fix it. The snapshot comes back with the reinstall.
  • Don't touch /System. It is sealed, and the space there is under "macOS", not System Data.
  • Don't run a "cleaner" that deletes ~/Library/Caches wholesale the week after an update. You are paying to rebuild caches twice.
