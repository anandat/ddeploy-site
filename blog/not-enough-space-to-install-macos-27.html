Typical sizes on Macs that hit the "not enough space" message. The commands below give your own numbers.

You clicked Upgrade Now on macOS 27 Golden Gate, or on 27.0.1 this week, and got the message that there isn't enough free space. Maybe Finder even says you have 30 GB free. Here is how much the update really needs, why the numbers disagree, and where to find the space in the order that frees the most for the least effort. Nothing here touches your documents, photos or music.

How much space macOS 27 needs

The installer is about 16 to 19 GB depending on whether it comes through Software Update or as a full installer. The install then needs room to unpack and to keep your current system until the new one boots. Plan on 35 to 40 GB free. There is one more thing people get caught by: after the first login, Apple Intelligence downloads its models, 14 to 30 GB on supported Macs, and macOS 27 has no switch to stop it. If you land at 2 GB free after the install, you will be full again within the day. More on that here.

First, the real number

Finder's "available" and Storage settings both include purgeable space: files macOS says it could remove if it had to, mostly local snapshots and iCloud copies. The installer can't always use it. To see what the disk can take right now:

df -h /

Look at the Avail column. If it is much lower than what Finder claims, step 1 is your answer.

1. Delete local snapshots (the biggest, fastest win)

Time Machine keeps hourly snapshots on the Mac itself, and macOS takes one before every update. Each pins files you have since deleted or changed. On a full disk they are often 20 GB or more.

tmutil listlocalsnapshots /

Delete each one by its date:

sudo tmutil deletelocalsnapshots 2026-09-28-101500

Your backups on the Time Machine drive are not affected. Leave any com.apple.os.update-… line alone. Details in Time Machine local snapshots.

2. Old macOS installers

An installer you downloaded yourself for Sonoma, Sequoia or Tahoe is still 12 to 19 GB in your Applications folder.

ls -d /Applications/Install\ macOS*.app

Keep Install macOS Golden Gate if that is the one you are about to run. Move the others to the Trash.

3. Old iPhone and iPad backups

Every device you have backed up to this Mac has a full backup in your Library, and the old ones are never removed. If you are also setting up a new iPhone this month, this is the one to look at. System Settings → General → Storage → iOS Files shows them with dates and sizes. Keep the latest backup of the phone you use; the rest can usually go. Walkthrough in Old iPhone backups on your Mac.

4. Your Downloads folder

Open Downloads in Finder, switch to list view and sort by Size. App installers (.dmg, .pkg) and .zip files you already opened are usually the biggest things there, and you will never open them again. This one is your call, which is why it is fourth.

5. Caches and logs

~/Library/Caches and ~/Library/Logs are safe to empty. Apps rebuild what they need, and some will be a little slower the first time you open them. Quit your apps first. Usually 2 to 10 GB.

6. If you write code

Developer Macs are where the space usually is. The three big ones:

xcrun simctl runtime list
docker system df
du -sh ~/Library/Developer/Xcode/DerivedData

Old simulator runtimes are 7 to 10 GB each, and Docker and DerivedData are rebuilt on demand. See simulator runtimes, Docker Desktop, DerivedData and project build folders. If you use Claude Code, check ~/.claude too; it can grow to hundreds of GB.

7. Empty the Trash, then restart

Nothing you moved to the Trash frees a byte until you empty it. Empty it, restart so macOS can settle its own bookkeeping, check df -h / again, then run Software Update.

What not to do

  • Don't erase the Mac to "make room". A clean install needs a full backup and a restore that puts everything back anyway.
  • Don't delete anything in /System or /Library/Updates. The first is sealed; the second is Software Update's own staging area and deleting it mid-download confuses it.
  • Don't let a cleaner wipe ~/Library/Application Support. That is where apps keep your data, not their caches.
  • Don't stop at 20 GB free. The install may squeeze through and leave you with a Mac too full to run well, before Apple Intelligence adds its share.
